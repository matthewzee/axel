# The first screen that converts

The first screen is what a phone shows before any scroll. For a local business it does one job: get the call or the booking from someone who needs the work now. Every site, landing page and mockup the team makes passes this check.

## What is on it
1. A call button, top right, that dials the business: the number visible, not a menu away.
2. One more button: book or get a quote, going to a short form (the skill "Build a trades intake form" (trades-intake-form)), never to a contact page.
3. The Google rating and review count, real, beside the buttons.
4. One line that says the trade, the area and the promise: "Plumber in Roxana. Same day, or the visit is free." A stranger gets it in two seconds.
5. One reason to call today: a time promise, a first visit price, same day service, a season offer with an end date. One, not three.
6. One real photo of them at work, or no photo. Never a stock photo, never a slider.

## What is not on it
1. A cookie wall, a popup, a chat bubble covering the button, a video that autoplays, a menu that opens by itself.
2. A slogan with no trade in it, a paragraph, a list of values, an award from years ago.
3. Anything that moves before the page settles, or that makes the button jump.

## When ads send people here
The page repeats the ad's offer in its first line, word for word, and the button does what the ad promised. One landing page per service the ads sell, each with its own first screen.

## Speed
The first screen draws in under 3 seconds on a phone connection: images sized for a phone, no slider, no heavy script above the fold, the fonts the phone already has or one font at most.

## Check it
Before anything is shown or deployed: Chrome's device toolbar at an iPhone size, load it fresh, and go down the list above. Then tap the call button and the book button and say what each did. A page that fails one point is not done.

## Only what is true
The rating, the count, the number, the promise and the services are the business's real ones, from their listing and the owner. Nothing invented, ever, even in a mockup. Never contact the business to get them: read their listing and ask the user.
